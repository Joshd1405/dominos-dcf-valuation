# Domino's Pizza Group plc — Equity Valuation Summary

**Retrospective FY2025 model | Reporting date: 28 December 2025 | Historical quoted comparator: 24 December 2025 (171.8p)**

## Valuation conclusion

A five-year FCFF discounted cash flow valuation produces an indicative **base equity value of 316.69p per share** (enterprise value **£1,531.37m**; equity value **£1,207.07m**). The model applies a **7.31% WACC** and **2.00% perpetual growth**, using **381.158m shares** after excluding EBT holdings. The indicated value is **84.3% above** the historical 171.8p reference, but this is **not evidence that the market was wrong**: the valuation uses FY2025 audited information published after that quoted price.

| Scenario | Indicative value | Economic drivers |
|---|---:|---|
| Downside | 236.24p | Weaker annual network growth (−1.0 pp), EBIT margin (−0.75 pp), higher CapEx (+1.0 pp of revenue) and working-capital cash use (+0.2 pp of revenue) |

*Scenario shocks operate on the base forecast; selected WACC and terminal growth remain unchanged.*

## Business and method

Domino's operates a franchised pizza network, earning revenue through its supply-chain and franchising activities. Store-network development and like-for-like sales underpin system sales; reported group revenue follows a different, related measure. The model forecasts FY2026–FY2030 operating results and FCFF, discounts the explicit-period cash flows, adds a Gordon-growth terminal value and bridges enterprise value to equity after adjusting for borrowing, cash, head leases and finance sublease receivables.

Selected modelling inputs include a 25% operating tax rate and FY2026–FY2030 CapEx/revenue of **5.00%, 4.50%, 4.25%, 4.00%, 4.00%**. The WACC reflects an equity beta derived from historical prices, a 4.51% gilt proxy, a 5.0% assumed equity risk premium and borrowing/lease financing costs.

## Major judgement: long-run economics

Of the modelled **£1,531.37m enterprise value**, around **£1,205.14m (78.7%)** is the present value of terminal cash flows and **£326.23m** is the PV of forecast-period FCFF. Valuation is therefore highly sensitive to sustainable post-2030 profitability, maintenance investment, working capital, refinancing and lease-renewal economics. A no-growth terminal benchmark and WACC/perpetual-growth grid are included in the workbook; no single terminal assumption should be treated as established fact.

A **reverse DCF** holding the five-year cash flows and selected discount/growth rates fixed suggests **£49.30m FY2031 terminal FCFF** is consistent with the 171.8p historical reference, compared with **£90.99m FY2031 FCFF** in the base model. This comparison highlights the burden of proof around long-run cash generation without asserting an observable market forecast.

## Assessment

**Strengths:** audited five-year historical foundation; transparent FCFF bridge; formula-driven operating scenarios; reconciled share count and lease/sublease bridge; dynamic discount-rate/growth sensitivity and reverse DCF; reviewed valuation arithmetic and model-input propagation.

**Limitations:** retrospective information timing; subjective cost of capital and reinvestment assumptions; simplified forward debt, lease-renewal and cash-flow sustainability forecasts; material dependence on terminal value. Forecast balance-sheet ties demonstrate internal consistency, **not** a fully detailed IFRS three-statement forecast.

**Overall conclusion:** The base DCF is best interpreted as a **conditional valuation under a specified operating and financing outlook**, not a price target. The chief analytical finding is the considerable effect of sustainable terminal FCFF and WACC assumptions on equity value.
